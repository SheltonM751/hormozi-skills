# Offer Audit: the Value Equation, applied

A working method for fixing an offer that isn't selling. It applies Hormozi's Value Equation one variable at a time.

Adapted from Kimia (@kimiabuilds), "How to create an irresistible offer". Her worked example was a paid crypto newsletter. Source formula: $100M Offers, Alex Hormozi.

```
Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        / (Time Delay x Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Make the top as big as possible. Make the bottom as small as possible.

## Core diagnosis

When sales are poor, the instinct is to add more. More features, more content, more bonuses. That raises the seller's cost, but buyers don't see any more value.

The fix is usually the opposite. Make the outcome clear, prove it, make it arrive sooner and make it easier. Then cut anything that doesn't help.

Kimia's result: she cut the features, and sales went up.

## The four variables

### 1. Dream Outcome: is it an outcome, and do they want it?

- Name the result the buyer gets, not the things they receive. A feature list is not an outcome.
- **"Then what?" test:** read the offer and ask "then what?" If the answer isn't a result the buyer cares about, you're selling inputs.
- Check that the buyer wants that result. Don't assume it.
- Change the product, not just the words. Move the delivery towards the outcome and drop what doesn't serve it.

Example. Before: "Weekly crypto newsletters, ebooks and giveaways." After: "Spot better trading opportunities with weekly crypto market analysis." She also added a weekly "coins to trade" section and dropped regulatory updates.

**Questions:**
1. What specific outcome does the customer want?
2. Does the offer say that outcome, or only list features?

### 2. Perceived Likelihood: why would they believe it works for them?

- Buyers choose the seller with more evidence. A coach with 500 results beats a coach with 4, even with the same promise.
- Use proof in numbers: how many people, what result, over what period.
- Social proof counts even before you have paid results. A large free audience shows people trust you.

Example. Added: "Join 6,000 crypto traders..."

**Questions:**
3. What proof do you have that the offer works?
4. Which testimonials, results or numbers would raise the buyer's confidence?

### 3. Time Delay: when do they get value?

Split this into two clocks:
- **Time to first value:** when they first feel progress. Make this day one.
- **Time to dream outcome:** when they reach the full result. This can take longer.

Losing the first 1kg matters even when the goal is 10kg. Give a quick win that also prepares them for the bigger result.

Example. Added instant access to a beginner course on day one. Without it, new buyers waited days for the first newsletter.

**Questions:**
5. How long does someone wait before getting any value?
6. What can you give them on day one?

### 4. Effort & Sacrifice: what must they start or stop doing?

- List what the buyer must **start** doing that they don't want to do.
- List what they must **stop** doing that they enjoy.
- Remove as many of these as you can. Every extra step is a reason not to buy.
- Promise a time cap (for example, "just 10 minutes a week"). It's a selling point and a rule for how you deliver. It makes you ask how to get the same result with less.
- Attach the effort to a habit they already have. Morning Brew did this with the morning coffee.

Example. Added: "All in just 10 minutes of reading a week."

**Questions:**
7. What does the customer have to do or give up to get the outcome?
8. How can you make that easier, faster or less demanding?

## Subtraction pass

Go through every part of the offer. For each one, ask which of the four variables it improves.

- It improves none of them? Cut it.
- It costs a lot and improves only a little? Cut it or swap it for something cheaper.

Kimia cut the giveaways, ebooks and private community. None of them moved a variable.

## Build the offer statement one layer at a time

Rewrite the offer one variable at a time. Show each version so the change is visible:

1. Outcome: "Spot better trading opportunities with weekly crypto market analysis."
2. + Proof: "Join 6,000 crypto traders spotting better trading opportunities..."
3. + Effort cap: "...All in just 10 minutes of reading a week."
4. + Day-one value: "...Plus instant access to our beginner trading course on day one."

## Process

1. Write down the current offer, word for word.
2. Answer the eight questions above.
3. Score each variable from 1 to 5. Name the weakest one: that's your biggest lever.
4. Run the subtraction pass.
5. Rewrite the offer statement one layer at a time.
6. Put the before and after side by side.
7. List what the product must change to make the new statement true. A new statement with an unchanged product is just new copy.
